Salt Creek / Striped Peak Legacy Forests

3 Steps to Take Right Now!

1) Learn More

We are asking the public to help stop two Washington DNR timber sales near Salt Creek Recreation Area and Striped Peak. Together they would log about 300 acres of rare coastal forest between Freshwater Bay and Crescent Beach, an area that sees about 20,000 visitors a year. Hundreds of people have also filled out a Clallam County parks survey supporting conservation of these forests.

Birds Eye View

About 181 acres of rare coastal lowland forest on a north-facing slope above the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Unit 1 would clear-cut nearly a mile of the 2.3-mile Strait Slope Trail. A globally critically imperiled ecosystem (G1/S1) has been documented here, along with a state-sensitive plant, Whipple-vine.

Tiger Stripes Sorts

About 123 acres, plus 2,850 feet of new forest roads. The timber rights were auctioned to Murphy Company for $1,168,544.46 in March 2026. The Washington Natural Heritage Program found one state-endangered ecosystem and two state-threatened ecosystems in the sale area.

2) Add Your Name

Please join our sign-on letter to the Commissioner of Public Lands and the Board of Natural Resources against logging the ‘Tiger Stripes Sorts’ and ‘Birds Eye View’ timber sales along the Striped Peak Loop / Strait Slope Trail near the popular Salt Creek Recreation Area. More than 1,000 people have already joined.
