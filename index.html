Lucia Buscemi

University of British Columbia Geography Master's student.

I study how landscape changes affect community livelihoods in the Everest region of Nepal. As an Environmental Anthropologist and Geographer, I am inherently interested in the connection between humans and their environment. I tell stories through photographs, film, and maps- combining spatial and visual elements to understand landscape change.

Research

Hazards, tourism, and livelihoods in the Himalaya, and how local communities respond to landscape changes.

Master's research at UBC, in progress, 2025–27

The Effects of Hazard-Related Landscape Changes on Livelihoods in Khumbu Pasanglhamu Rural Municipality, Nepal

How floods and landslides affect livelihoods in Solukhumbu. Designed with the Sagarmatha Pollution Control Committee and Khumbu Pasanglhamu Rural Municipality. Supervisor: Dr. Michele Koppes, Committee: Dr. Phurwa Dolpopa.

Fulbright research, 2024–25

Tourism infrastructure, climate adaptation, and policy

Ethnographic research in the Everest region on trekking trails, adaptation initiatives, and government policy, alongside waste management work including the Carry Me Back program.

Honors thesis, 2023

Footsteps on the Roof of the World: Navigating Anthropogenic Impacts in the Everest Region

Supported by a Halle Institute research fellowship and awarded highest honors. It looks at the autonomy of Khumbu residents and challenges Western media portrayals of trash in the region.

Research thesis, Chile, 2024

Las Hierbas Medicinales y Problemas Ambientales: Socio-Political-Environmental Nexus of the Mapuche Population in Central-Southern Chile

A capstone thesis about the relationship between dictator-era forest plantations in Chile with the Indigenous Mapuche's relationship with/access to medicinal plants.
